Independence did not come from one movement or one leader.
Many currents, which often disagreed with each other, worked against British rule over six decades, and their relative importance is still argued over [@src.chandra-1989-indias-struggle-for-independence; @src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].

## Early nationalism

The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885.
For its first two decades it was dominated by English-educated lawyers and professionals who petitioned for a larger Indian role in government.
After the partition of Bengal in 1905, a more assertive current, with leaders such as Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal and Lala Lajpat Rai, adopted boycott of British goods and the call for swaraj [@src.chandra-1989-indias-struggle-for-independence].
The All-India Muslim League was formed in 1906, and the reforms of 1909 created separate electorates for Muslims, which shaped later politics [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].

## Gandhi and mass politics

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi returned to India from South Africa in 1915, where he had developed satyagraha, the method of resisting injustice through nonviolent action.
After 1919 he led Congress in mobilising peasants, workers and townspeople on a scale earlier leaders had not [@src.brown-1989-gandhi-prisoner-of-hope].

The immediate trigger was repression.
The Rowlatt Act of 1919 extended wartime emergency powers, and on 13 April 1919 troops under Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer fired on an unarmed crowd in the Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar.
The official British inquiry counted 379 dead, while Indian estimates were substantially higher; Kim Wagner's study places the event in the climate of fear that officials felt in the Punjab [@src.wagner-2019-amritsar-1919].

The Non-Cooperation Movement (1920 to 1922), which Congress adopted alongside the Khilafat campaign, asked Indians to boycott British courts, schools and cloth.
Gandhi called it off in February 1922 after a mob killed police officers at Chauri Chaura [@src.brown-1989-gandhi-prisoner-of-hope; @src.chandra-1989-indias-struggle-for-independence].

In March 1930 Gandhi led a march of about 24 days from Sabarmati to the coast at Dandi and broke the salt law there on 6 April, which started the Civil Disobedience Movement.
Salt was taxed and affected every household, so the protest was easy to understand and difficult to defend [@src.brown-1989-gandhi-prisoner-of-hope].
The movement was suspended after the Gandhi-Irwin Pact of 1931, and resumed in 1932 [@src.chandra-1989-indias-struggle-for-independence].

Gandhi's leadership was also criticised from within.
B. R. Ambedkar, for example, disagreed with Congress on the political representation of Dalits, which led to the Poona Pact of 1932 [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].

## Revolutionaries and the armed path

A separate tradition took up political violence.
The Hindustan Socialist Republican Association, to which Bhagat Singh belonged, shot a police officer, J. P. Saunders, in Lahore in December 1928 and threw bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly in 1929.
Bhagat Singh was hanged in March 1931 and became a lasting symbol of resistance, though his socialist and atheist views are often left out of later popular memory [@src.chandra-1989-indias-struggle-for-independence].

Subhas Chandra Bose, who had been elected president of Congress, left it after disagreeing with Gandhi over strategy.
In 1943 he took command of the Indian National Army, which had first been formed in 1942 in Southeast Asia from Indian prisoners of war captured by Japan, and he proclaimed a Provisional Government of Free India [@src.bose-2011-his-majestys-opponent].
The INA fought alongside Japanese forces and was defeated in the Burma campaign.
The trials of INA officers in Delhi in 1945 and 1946 provoked wide public sympathy across religious lines [@src.bose-2011-his-majestys-opponent; @src.chandra-1989-indias-struggle-for-independence].

## Quit India and the war years

When the Second World War began, the Viceroy declared India at war without consulting Indian leaders, and Congress ministries resigned.
In August 1942, after the failure of the Cripps Mission, Congress passed the Quit India resolution in Bombay, and its leaders were arrested the next day.
Without them, protests and sabotage spread, and the government suppressed them with force [@src.chandra-1989-indias-struggle-for-independence; @src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].
Historians are divided on how much Quit India itself weakened British rule, compared with the pressure of war, the cost of the empire and Britain's changed position after 1945 [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].

## Pakistan and Partition

Muslim political opinion was divided on separate nationhood.
The Muslim League, under Muhammad Ali Jinnah, adopted the Lahore Resolution in 1940, calling for Muslim-majority areas to be grouped as "independent states", and it won most Muslim seats in the elections of 1945 and 1946 [@src.khan-2007-the-great-partition].
The Cabinet Mission plan of 1946 for a loose federation failed, and communal violence in Calcutta in August 1946 and elsewhere hardened positions [@src.khan-2007-the-great-partition].

The last Viceroy, Lord Mountbatten, announced in June 1947 that British India would be divided, with the date of transfer set for mid-August, much earlier than previously planned.
Pakistan became a dominion on 14 August 1947 and India on 15 August.
The boundary awards for Punjab and Bengal were announced only after independence [@src.khan-2007-the-great-partition].

Partition brought mass killing and displacement.
Estimates of the dead range widely, from a couple of hundred thousand to as many as two million, and more than ten million people were displaced, one of the largest forced migrations of the twentieth century [@src.khan-2007-the-great-partition].
Historians argue about whether responsibility lies mainly with the Muslim League's demand, with Congress's choices, with the speed of the British withdrawal, or with the longer history of communal politics, and Yasmin Khan emphasises the contingency of events rather than inevitability [@src.khan-2007-the-great-partition].

## How historians read the movement

Writers in the nationalist tradition, such as Bipan Chandra and his co-authors, present the movement as a broad anti-colonial struggle that built a unified nation across classes [@src.chandra-1989-indias-struggle-for-independence].
Other schools question this: some emphasise elite competition and the imperial context, and others have written about the peasants, workers, women and tribal groups whose actions did not always follow Congress's lead [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].
A reader should treat any account that attributes independence to a single person or method as a simplification.
