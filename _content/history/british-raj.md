"Raj" is a Hindi and Urdu word meaning rule.
Strictly, the British Raj refers to the period of direct Crown government from 1858 to 1947.
British power in India began a century earlier and was built first by a trading company [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].

## From trade to territory

The English East India Company was founded in 1600 and traded from coastal settlements for more than a century.
As the Mughal Empire weakened in the eighteenth century, regional states became more independent and the Company, with its own armies, began to take part in their politics [@src.dalrymple-2019-the-anarchy].

At Plassey in June 1757 the Company's forces under Robert Clive defeated the Nawab of Bengal, Siraj ud-Daula, largely because key commanders had been bribed to stay out of the fight.
After the Battle of Buxar in 1764, the Mughal emperor Shah Alam II granted the Company the diwani (the right to collect revenue) of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa in 1765 [@src.dalrymple-2019-the-anarchy; @src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].
This gave a private company the revenues of a rich province and the means to pay for a larger army, which it used in successive wars against Mysore, the Marathas and others [@src.dalrymple-2019-the-anarchy].

## The uprising of 1857 and the transfer to the Crown

In 1857 sepoys of the Company's Bengal Army rebelled at Meerut, and the uprising spread through the north and centre, joined by civilians, landholders and deposed rulers.
It was suppressed by 1858 after heavy fighting and reprisals [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].
Historians disagree about how to describe it.
The British called it the Mutiny, and nationalist writers, beginning with V. D. Savarkar in 1909, called it a "war of independence".
Many scholars now prefer "rebellion" or "uprising" because it began as a military revolt and its aims and participants varied greatly by region [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].

Parliament then passed the Government of India Act of 1858, ending Company rule.
Queen Victoria's proclamation of November 1858 promised not to extend territory and to respect religious practice, and the Crown took over the Company's possessions and treaties with princely states [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].
A large minority of the subcontinent by area stayed under princely rulers who accepted British paramountcy, while the rest was governed as British India [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].

## Government and ideas of reform

The Raj governed through a small, largely British civil service, an army with a heavy Indian component, and alliances with landholders and princes.
Law, courts, surveys and censuses expanded, and the census in particular shaped how Indians were classified, including by caste and religion [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].

Reform was a frequent theme in British language, and real laws were passed.
The Bengal Sati Regulation of 1829, under Governor-General William Bentinck, made the burning of widows illegal.
Lata Mani's study of the debate shows that Indian reformers like Rammohan Roy took part, and that the argument was conducted around what scriptures said and what "tradition" required, with the women themselves rarely heard [@src.mani-1998-contentious-traditions].
Thomas Macaulay's Minute of 1835 favoured English-language higher education, and the policy created a class of English-educated Indians who later became central to nationalist politics [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].

## The economy: what the evidence shows

Railways began running in 1853 and the network grew into one of the largest in the world, alongside telegraph lines and irrigation canals [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].
Historians debate who benefited: the lines connected ports to the interior to move raw materials for export and troops across the country, and the cost and the equipment were largely paid for by Indian revenues [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].

The idea that British policy "de-industrialised" India is contested.
Prasannan Parthasarathi has argued that in the eighteenth century South Asian textiles were competitive worldwide, and that British protection of its own industry and the mechanisation of cotton production changed that balance [@src.parthasarathi-2011-why-europe-grew-rich].
Others stress that the effect varied by region and industry and that handloom production did not disappear. The question of how much is attributable to policy and how much to technology is still being argued [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].

## Famine and its causes

Severe famines struck repeatedly, including in the 1870s and the 1890s.
Mike Davis argues that colonial policies, free-market ideology and rigid relief rules made famines worse, and that the El Nino droughts that triggered them were turned into mass deaths by how the state responded [@src.davis-2001-late-victorian-holocausts].
Amartya Sen's analysis of the Bengal famine of 1943 found that food was available in the province, but that wartime inflation, disruption of trade and the loss of purchasing power of the poor meant that millions of people could not buy it [@src.sen-1981-poverty-and-famines].
Not all historians accept every part of these arguments, but they are central to the modern discussion of the colonial record [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].

## Resistance and the end

The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885 and began as a body that petitioned for reform.
The partition of Bengal in 1905, carried out by the Viceroy Lord Curzon, set off large protests and the swadeshi movement, and it was annulled in 1911 [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].
After the First World War, mass movements, covered in a separate article, changed what the Raj could expect from its subjects, and the Second World War left Britain unable to sustain the empire.
The Raj ended with the Indian Independence Act of 1947, which created two new dominions, India and Pakistan [@src.metcalf-2012-concise-history-modern-india].
